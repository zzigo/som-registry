The concept of Infinite Scroll was born in 2006 when **Aza Raskin** was working on the Humanized Reader app. He envisioned a seamless way for users to consume content without the need to click through page after page (pagination).

The goal was to reduce interaction and create a seamless user experience. He suggested removing the pagination from the Google search results, which he found annoying and aggravating when trying to remember which page had the result the user was looking for.

Initially used on Flickr, a photo-sharing platform, the concept quickly gained traction for its seamless browsing experience and endless content discovery.

Both jump cuts and infinite scroll go beyond simply grabbing attention. They are used to:

- **Condense information:** By quickly transitioning between different scenes or pieces of content, editors, and designers can convey more information in less time.
- **Heighten emotional impact:** Jump cuts create a sense of urgency, excitement, or even disorientation, while infinite scrolls can build anticipation and curiosity.
- **Direct attention:** Both techniques guide the viewer's focus to specific elements or moments, ensuring they don't miss important information.
