## Organological Summary

The GD Violin (2022) is, by Autodesk's account, the first instrument whose body was generated by Fusion 360 generative design. Luthier Kevin Baslé worked with Autodesk research engineer Peter Storey at the Autodesk Technology Center in Birmingham, UK. The workflow modeled only the strictly necessary parts — where strings and standard fittings attach, and where the violinist holds the instrument — and let the generative engine maximize freedom and minimize mass, producing organic, nature-like forms. A chosen solution was 3D-printed as a prototype, then CNC-milled from a single block of wood and hand-finished.

## The decoupling thesis (why this node matters to SOOG)

The acoustic [[violin]] sits at O=0.9: four centuries of refinement make its body so optimal that the object IS its function — millimeters of plate thickness change the sound. The GD Violin inverts exactly this. Because it is electric, the body's shape and volume have almost no effect on the sound, so morphology is acoustically free. The instrument therefore separates two things the acoustic violin fuses:

$$ \text{acoustic violin:}\quad \text{form} \equiv \text{sound} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \text{GD violin:}\quad \text{form} \perp \text{sound} $$

This orthogonality is the organologically interesting move. When form no longer carries the acoustic burden, it becomes available for other determinations: ergonomics, mass, identity, manufacturability, expression. Generative design fills that freed space with force-and-constraint-derived form. As Storey notes, the craft still matters — "without Baslé's expert craft, it would not sound the way it does" — so the luthier's hand is not eliminated but relocated to finishing and setup.

## Acoustic / signal chain

```
bow (horsehair — rosin contact)
    ↓ [Helmholtz stick-slip: bow excites string]

    ↓ [electromagnetic / piezo pickup — body is acoustically inert]
amplifier / signal chain
    ↓
loudspeaker / room
```

Body radiation is negligible (electric); the body's role is structural and ergonomic, not acoustic. `recursive: false`.

## Relevance to SOOG

This case is a concrete, mainstream precedent for [[fusion 360 — MOC|P7 Synthesis under Constraints]] and [[fusion 360 — MOC|P9 Manufacturability as Generative Cause]] applied to an instrument: the maker authors the constraint space (anchor points, hold zones, material, CNC process) and the system synthesizes the body. It also exposes a SOOG design principle worth naming — morphological freedom under acoustic decoupling: in electric/transduced instruments, the object axis (O) is liberated from the interaction axis (I), so generative and speculative form-making can proceed without acoustic penalty. For acoustic instruments the same move is impossible — there, form re-enters as acoustic cause and [[nTop — case study|nTop's material-field]] approach becomes the relevant tool.

Contrast pair for the MOAIE space: [[violin]] (form ≡ sound, O=0.9) vs GD Violin (form ⊥ sound, O=0.55). The grammar distance between them is a single decoupling operator (electric transduction) plus a generative-form operator.
