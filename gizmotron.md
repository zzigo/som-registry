In the days before sampling, [10cc](https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/artists/f37c537b-3557-4031-bfd6-ab63ced32854 "BBC Music - 10cc")’s quest to cheaply reproduce the sound of an orchestral string section led them to invent the Gizmotron - a device that clamped across the strings of an electric guitar, its small motor-driven plastic wheels providing a hypnotic sustain effect. 10cc used the Gizmotron widely on 1974’s Sheet Music and its two subsequent albums. Drunk on possibility, Kevin Godley and Lol Creme [then quit the band to develop the instrument](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p039nxgr "BBC Four - 10cc - "I probably did too much talking and not enough strangling""), showcasing its charms on their 1977 triple album Consequences. Mass production commenced, yet despite further exposure on albums by [Wings](https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/artists/d922d727-240a-4432-9a88-05a7cf9bc403 "BBC Music - Wings") and [Led Zeppelin](https://www.bbc.co.uk/music/artists/678d88b2-87b0-403b-b63d-5da7465aecc3 "BBC Music - Led Zepellin"), the Gizmotron proved unreliable and ended up bankrupting its manufacturer, Musitronics. Luckily, there’s a happy ending - in 2013, a new team of engineers took up the concept and you can now buy your very own Gizmotron 2.0 for $289.99.
