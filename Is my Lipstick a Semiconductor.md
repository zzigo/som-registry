## Organological Summary

*Is My Lipstick a Semiconductor?* (Ioana Vreme Moser, iii residency 2025; the installation *Arsenic Ballerinas*, commissioned by CTM Festival / DAAD / iii, shown at CTM 2026) treats cosmetics as electronic matter. Its constitutive property is a material identity claim: the heavy metals that make lipstick toxic — lead, arsenic, mercury, and above all galena (lead sulphide) — are the same minerals that demodulated the first radio signals and seeded the semiconductor industry. Moser excavates historical cosmetic recipes, sets crushed galena and iron oxides through LC oscillation circuits to produce tones, and rebuilds 133 historical lipsticks into functional FM receivers — antenna sealed in the coloured wax, circuit and loudspeaker in the case — suspended as a swarm that catches local airwaves and speaks back the entangled histories of cosmetics, radio, toxicity, war, and the uncredited "Young Lady" radio operators. For speculative organology it is the limit case of the M dimension and a model of critical organology: the "instrument" is not a built sound-object but the electrical behaviour of an unexpected material, and the work asks whether an organology can classify a thing whose claim is precisely that the cosmetic and the semiconductor are the same substance.

---

## Formal Structure

The work's physics is the physics of the crystal radio, with a cosmetic mineral standing in for the manufactured component.

1. The material as semiconductor. Galena (PbS) is a natural semiconductor, band gap $E_g \approx 0.41\ \text{eV}$. A metal point contact on galena forms a rectifying (Schottky-like) junction — the historical "cat's-whisker" detector. The work's thesis is that the same mineral, present as a cosmetic pigment, retains this junction behaviour: the material is the diode.

2. Envelope detection (the lipstick as receiver). An amplitude-modulated carrier picked up by the antenna is
$$
v_{\text{RF}}(t) = A\,[\,1 + m\,s(t)\,]\cos(\omega_c t),
$$
with carrier $\omega_c$, modulation depth $m$, message $s(t)$. The galena junction rectifies (passes one polarity), and an $RC$ low-pass with $RC \gg 1/\omega_c$ but $RC \ll 1/\omega_s$ removes the carrier, recovering the audio envelope
$$
v_{\text{audio}}(t) \;\propto\; A\,m\,s(t).
$$
No power supply is required for the detection itself — the broadcast carries the energy. The lipstick literally speaks with the city's airwaves.

3. Tone generation (the Bad Girl Organ). Crushed galena and iron oxides placed in an $LC$ tank set a resonance
$$
f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}},
$$
and with the mineral junction providing nonlinearity / negative resistance the circuit oscillates, producing the "strident" tones; in the lecture-performance these lipsticks also perturb the control voltages of a modular synthesiser. A conductive cosmetic medium (an eleventh-century egg-white-plus-mineral recipe from *The Trotula*) closes the circuit.

So the signal chain is
$$
\text{cosmetic mineral (PbS, oxides)} \;\rightarrow\; \text{junction / LC behaviour} \;\rightarrow\; \text{demodulation or oscillation} \;\rightarrow\; \text{loudspeaker},
$$
i.e. material property → circuit behaviour → sound. The instrument is upstream of any object: it is the electrical disposition of the matter itself.

## Material as Ontological Claim (Critical Organology)

Every other node in the catalogue uses material as a means to a sound. Here the material *is the argument*, and the sound is its evidence.

| cosmetic mineral | toxic role | electronic role | the work's point |
|---|---|---|---|
| galena (PbS) | lead poisoning | first radio detector / semiconductor | the poison is the technology |
| iron oxides | pigment | LC-circuit element → tones | the colour sounds |
| mercury / arsenic | "devilish smears," cosmetics | dopants, semiconductor history | beauty and the chip share a supply chain |
| radium / thorium (historical) | radioactive cosmetics (Tho-Radia) | radio-luminescence | the face that "glows from within" is irradiated |

The lipstick's bullet form folds the chain shut: cosmetic → munition → radio bug. The 133 receivers in the swarm become "audio surveillance bugs, spying on their own history," voicing fragments of Young Ladies Radio League testimonies, cosmetic commercials, and pre-war songs. This is why the node's layer is `ontological`, not `acoustic`: it is validated not by a dispersion relation but by the coherence of a material-political claim. It is the catalogue's clearest instance of *critical organology* — organology practised as a argument about matter, gender, and technology rather than as instrument design.

## MOAIE Reading

Vector: **[0.95, 0.60, 0.50, 0.65, 0.65]**, recursive = false.

**M = 0.95** — The defining score and the M-ceiling of the catalogue. Where the daxophone's M = 0.70 means "material determines timbre," here M means "material is the instrument's entire ontology": the work's title is a question *about the material's electrical identity*, and the answer is the sound. Nothing higher is meaningful short of the fictional Mnemosynophone (M = 1.0, material = crystallised memory).

**O = 0.60** — Moderate. The object is a found cosmetic case rebuilt as a receiver and multiplied into a swarm; the topology is real and distinctive but secondary to the material claim.

**A = 0.50** — Hybrid. The artist performs, assembles, and curates; the installation then runs autonomously, receiving whatever the local FM spectrum carries. Human and environmental agency share the work — hence the mid value rather than the Spectral Parrot's machine-dominant 0.90.

**I = 0.65** — Direct and critical-performative: material property maps straight to sound, and the bodily performance (applying, destroying, licking-but-not-licking the toxic lipstick) is part of the interaction. Above the violin, below the autonomous-agent instruments.

**E = 0.65** — The local FM spectrum is inside the signal chain; the work sounds with the city's airwaves and the history carried on them. Constitutive, though not a closed feedback loop (recursive = false).

Catalogue placement: this node and the daxophone share the high-M axis but mean different things by M (ontology vs timbre), and this node sits almost opposite the aeolian harp (max-E/min-M) and the Spectral Parrot (max-A/low-M). Plotted, the four real instruments now span M, A, and E near-independently — early evidence that the dimensions are doing separable work.

## Existing context (preserved)

iii residency (04.2025–05.2025). Ioana Vreme Moser (b. 1994, RO) uses rough electronic processes to obtain different "materialities of sound," placing fluidic/electrical currents in interaction with her body, minerals, and found items. The project examines the mineralogy of lipsticks, blushes, and powders — their inherited toxicity and radiopotencies (lead, arsenic, cinnabar, radium); the lipstick takes the bullet form, and heavy metals migrate "from our lips to our computers." *Arsenic Ballerinas* traces the entanglement of cosmetics with the semiconductor industry: substances distilled, waxes melted, powders pressed, high voltage applied, the controversial red lipstick made to resonate in strident sound. The "Bad Girl Organ" prototype uses toxic semiconductive pigments to generate noise and alter voltages in a modular synth. The installation suspends 133 lipstick-radios catching local FM, voicing seven transmissions drawn from Young Ladies Radio League *Harmonics*, cosmetic commercials, the artist's Coquetta character, and pre-war songs.

SOM note: instrument-as-circuit — the "instrument" is the electrical behaviour of an unexpected material, not a built sound-object; a case of critical / speculative organology.
