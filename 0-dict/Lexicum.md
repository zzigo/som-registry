## post-critic science
Post-critic science, also known as post-normal science, is a concept that challenges the traditional idea of scientific objectivity and involves incorporating multiple perspectives and values into scientific inquiry. It recognizes that many complex issues, such as climate change or genetic engineering, cannot be resolved solely through objective facts but require input from various stakeholders.
In this approach to science, societal considerations and ethical dimensions are given equal weight alongside empirical evidence. Post-critic scientists believe that scientific investigations should involve not only scientists but also politicians, affected communities, industry representatives, and others who have a stake in the issue under investigation.
Post-critic science aims to address uncertainty and value conflicts more explicitly by including all relevant actors’ insights from different disciplines to reach comprehensive decisions. It acknowledges that scientific knowledge is often uncertain due to incomplete data or multiple interpretations of evidence. Therefore it seeks diverse perspectives in order to provide decision-makers with a broad range of options based on different trade-offs.
Critics argue against post-critic science suggesting it undermines the authoritative nature of research since decisions are taken based on interests rather than facts alone. They claim that these inclusive processes can introduce bias or slow down decision-making.
Overall though, post-critic science proponents argue that by considering diverse viewpoints and values in scientific discussions they create better-informed policies while acknowledging their limitations due to subjective influence—and hence create opportunities for improving transparency around those influences which they see being able reduce conflicts between experts and society making progress towards civic engagement fruitful.
