# Playability

The ease with which a performer can enter and navigate the instrument's sound-production space. High playability means low threshold for producing intentional, controlled sound; low playability means high technical demands before any meaningful control is achieved.

Playability is not the same as expressivity: the toy xylophone has high playability (anyone can hit a note) but low expressivity (limited acoustic range and nuance). The violin has low playability (years before consistent tone production) but exceptional expressivity once mastered.

For new instrument design, playability sets the access condition — the prerequisite for all other interaction qualities to manifest. A non-playable instrument cannot demonstrate liveness, expressivity, or sensorimotor scheme.
