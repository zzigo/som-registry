# Tip-Surface Coupling

An excitation mode in which a mechanically driven tip engages intermittently with a resonating surface, converting a linear electromagnetic input into a nonlinear acoustic output through controlled contact geometry.

## Mechanism

Voice-coil motors (Lorentz force transducers) drive a bridge structure. The bridge terminates in a tip whose displacement amplitude is controlled by the input signal. When the tip amplitude exceeds a threshold determined by the gap geometry, it makes contact with the soundboard surface.

The contact is intermittent: the tip alternately engages and disengages with the surface at the driving frequency. This converts a sinusoidal excitation into a pulse-train-like interaction, which is acoustically rich and spectrally complex.

$$x(t) = A\sin(\omega t), \quad \text{contact when } x(t) > x_{\text{gap}}$$

The resulting acoustic output contains the fundamental $\omega$ plus a harmonic series generated by the nonlinear clipping-like contact event, whose spectrum depends on the gap, the tip material, and the soundboard impedance.

## Control Parameters

| Parameter | Control mode | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Excitation amplitude A | Touch pressure / CV | Contact duration per cycle, harmonic richness |
| Driving frequency ω | CV input | Fundamental pitch, modulation rate |
| Gap geometry x_gap | Physical setup (bridge height) | Onset threshold, nonlinear regime entry |
| Soundboard material | Physical swap | Filter characteristics of resonant body |

## Organological Position

This excitation mode does not appear in the Sachs-Hornbostel classification. It is not blown, bowed, plucked, struck, or friction-based. It occupies a distinct position in excitation space: an electronically mediated, threshold-crossing, surface-contact nonlinear coupling. The closest analogue is bowing (continuous contact under varying normal force) but the mechanism is categorically different — bowing relies on stick-slip friction; tip-surface coupling relies on gap-crossing impact at electromagnetic frequencies.

The operational principle abstracted from this excitation is [[nonlinear-contact-modulation]], which generalises across any instrument using threshold-mediated surface contact as a sound-generation mechanism.
