# Affordance

Following Gibson (1979) and developed for musical instruments by Tullberg (2022), affordances are the <mark class="hltr-green">action possibilities</mark> that the instrument-environment system offers to a particular performer. 
- They are <mark class="hltr-blue">relational</mark> — not a property of the instrument alone, nor of the performer alone, but of their dynamic coupling within a musical niche.

Tullberg's key argument: affordances of musical instruments must be understood as **amodal** (not confined to a single sense modality) and **sensorimotor** (emerging through the performer's continuous bodily engagement with the instrument, not from cognitive analysis of its properties). The unit of analysis is the sensorimotor relationship situated within a musical niche — not the object, not the subject, but the transaction between them.

Canonical affordances (Costall 1997) are the normatively expected action possibilities associated with instrument type: a clarinet affords holding in two hands, blowing through the mouthpiece, pressing keys. But affordances are skill-relative (Noë 2004): an advanced performer perceives affordances invisible to a beginner.
