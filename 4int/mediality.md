# Mediality

The instrument as mediating body: the particular capacity of a musical instrument to position itself between the performer's physical action and the acoustic result, functioning neither as pure tool (fully transparent) nor as pure medium (fully opaque), but as what Burrows (1987:117) calls a "transitional object" — simultaneously body and not-body, material and immaterial.

## Theoretical Basis

Burrows identifies mediation as the primary feature distinguishing musical instruments from other sound-producing devices: the instrument "mediates between apparently contrary realms, namely between corporeality and fluidity, between the inside and the outside and between the material and the immaterial" (Burrows 1987:117, cited in Hardjowirogo 2017:15).

Auslander extends this by noting that the instrument possesses "its own agency with which the musician must negotiate" — the instrument is not passive; it pushes back. This dialectical resistance is constitutive of mediation: the mediating object shapes the action that passes through it.

Alperson (2007:46) raises the boundary dissolution question: "it is difficult to know where the instrument ends and the rest of the body begins." Highly mediated instruments (synthesizers, laptops) make this boundary sharp and clear; highly integrated instruments (violin, flute) blur it completely through years of sensorimotor integration.

## Mediation Gradient

| Degree | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | Fully mediated — gesture triggers DSP, no mechanical body | Laptop + DAW |
| 0.3 | Mediated electronic — electromagnetic transduction, body visible but acoustically detached | Electroacoustic instrument (electromagnetic transduction) |
| 0.5 | Mechanical mediation — body action drives acoustic body through mechanism | Piano (hammer action) |
| 0.7 | Direct coupling — body in contact with resonating body | Guitar (plucked string) |
| 1.0 | Prosthetic — instrument felt as body extension | Violin (long-term player) |

Note: mediation value is not fixed per instrument type but varies with performer integration level — it is a relational, not dispositional, property (Tullberg 2022:2).

## Relation to Instrumentality

Mediality is one of Hardjowirogo's six criteria of instrumentality. Low mediality (high electronic mediation) produces the "boring laptop performer" problem (Hardjowirogo 2017:16): the causal chain from gesture to sound is invisible, undermining the audience's recognition of the system as a musical instrument. Design of new instruments must therefore deliberately manage mediality — either making the mediation visible (showing physical action that drives the electronics) or achieving such integration that mediation disappears into prosthetic extension.
